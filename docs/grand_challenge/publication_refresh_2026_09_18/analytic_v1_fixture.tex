\section{Explicit countermodels: algebraic closure without physical validity}
\label{app:gc-countermodels}

Numerical agreement between a transfer matrix and its Gaussian completion
cannot repair an inconsistent optical model. To make this distinction
quantitative, consider the diagnostic variant that retains a duplicate
manifold population factor and uses the opposite symmetric-frame mismatch
sign. Its static drift and transfer are
\begin{align}
 M_D={}&\begin{pmatrix}
 -1.330328+242.349727\ii&-0.410000+195.707754\ii\\
 0.693784-195.680004\ii&0.341352-171.013770\ii
 \end{pmatrix}\,\mathrm{m}^{-1},\label{eq:gc-countermodel-drift}\\
 T_D={}&\begin{pmatrix}
 -0.376876+2.339296\ii&-0.929014+1.950994\ii\\
 0.931714-1.949376\ii&1.593377-1.777683\ii
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \label{eq:gc-countermodel-transfer}
\end{align}
Here equal photon energies and equal mode areas are approximated, so $Q$
is proportional to the identity. The two squared transfer coefficients are
5.61434 and 4.66816, with gap 0.946182. Its commutator-defect matrix $K$
has eigenvalues 0.189391 and 3.153969. Omitting a reservoir gives a global
commutator residual 0.0551; the independent-vacuum completion reduces the
local and global residuals to $4.44\times10^{-16}$ and $1.04\times10^{-15}$.
These accurate identities do not validate the duplicate population or
mismatch sign.

The ideal comparison has conjugate gain 4.61434 and therefore is a different
channel. The static completion gives the same values at $0.1,0.15,2,4\MHz$,
so it cannot explain a spectral bandwidth or an electronic-noise peak.
Local logarithmic sensitivities of this variant are
$\partial\ln G_p/\partial\ln\ell_s\simeq3.53$ and
$\partial\ln G_c/\partial\ln\ell_s\simeq4.29$. Changing its coherence-only
ground damping from 90 to $110\kHz$ moves $G_p$ from 5.6138 to 5.6033 and
$G_c$ from 4.6631 to 4.6573. These derivatives are conditional on the
inconsistent variant and do not determine uncertainty in the reciprocal
model. Against approximate comparison gains 15.5 and 13.6, its discrepancies
would be $-63.8\%$ and $-65.7\%$; the exact raw-power experimental comparison
uses the separately specified seed powers in Table~\ref{tab:discrepancies}.

The gain-only covariance ansatz
\begin{equation}
 C_{pc}^{\rm ansatz}=\tfrac12\left[(G_p+g_c^{(\Phi)})
                    -(G_p-g_c^{(\Phi)})^2\right]
 \label{eq:gc-gain-covariance-ansatz}
\end{equation}
forces the scalar score in Eq.~\eqref{eq:ids-legacy}; it does not derive
an ordered atomic spectrum or ensure uncertainty/CP constraints. Negative
diagnostic values such as $-8.10$ or $-15.62\dB$ computed from gain alone
remain scores under that ansatz. The passive example $(G_p,g_c^{(\Phi)})=
(0.2,0.1)$ already falsifies its use as a universal squeezing formula.
Clipping the score into $[0,1]$ cannot supply missing vacuum noise.
