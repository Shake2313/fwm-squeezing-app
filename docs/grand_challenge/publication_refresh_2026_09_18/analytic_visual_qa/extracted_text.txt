Scope and status. This self-contained study separates semiclassical gain, microscopic quan-
tum fluctuations, transported atomic ensembles, and external detection. It derives the atomic
GKSL drift and ordered diffusion, their independent quantum regression reference, recipro-
cal field normalization, physical sideband channels, periodic and spatial response, finite-age
boundary noise, thermal inflow and nonlocal propagation requirements. These constructions
yield conditional spectra for declared reduced models. They do not yet establish a converged
thermal-vapor cell spectrum or quantitative apparatus agreement.
The phenomenological gain model and reciprocal microscopic model are distinct model vari-
ants. The former gives Gp = 403.53550 at one measured operating point and substantially
disagrees with the measured output powers. A gain-only score or an independent-vacuum
completion is not a microscopic squeezing prediction. The thermal inflow calculation passes
selected path accuracy gates but all six directed comparisons between three coarse ensem-
ble samples fail a 5% gate; source-resolved differences reach 43.61%. Boundary preparation,
ensemble refinement, nonlocal Maxwell closure, full Zeeman physics and measured detector
inputs remain material conditions on any apparatus claim. All numerical values below state
the model and test to which they belong.
Abstract
We develop a precision theory of seeded warm-vapor 85Rb D1 double-Λ four-wave mixing
from the density-matrix generator to SQL-normalized detection. The stationary response is a
projected Liouvillian resolvent, with a simple-pole expansion for diagonalizable generators and
a Jordan/contour qualification at defective points.
The distributed field-noise integral is an
entire function of a Lyapunov superoperator, evaluated without singular eigenvector coordinates.
Explicit conventions separate angular and ordinary frequencies, optical and analysis detunings,
power and photon-flux gain, and atomic from spatial field diffusion.
Microscopic ordered diffusion follows from the adjoint GKSL product rule.
Its resolvent
spectrum is independently checked by full-density-matrix quantum regression. Reciprocal dipole
normalization exposes the limitations of a common scalar hyperfine factor in a pumped four-state
surrogate. Physical positive/negative sidebands, Gaussian complete positivity, finite RF filters,
periodic seeds, two moving optical phases and finite spatial collection modes are treated explicitly.
Ballistic atoms carry initial quantum noise and jump noise across positions; Poisson entry adds
number noise. An adjoint trajectory construction supplies a source-resolved reference, while exact
constant-step convolutions and a fourth-order composition supply controlled path numerics.
Numerical examples distinguish admissibility, discretization convergence and apparatus vali-
dation. The atomic Raman half-width 0.520 MHz at one zero- velocity operating point and the
angular Doppler rms width 1.38 MHz do not determine a squeezing bandwidth. A positive fi-
nite Floquet state can still have a 16.8% gain truncation error. Removing transported boundary
noise can retain PSD noise while changing a spectrum by 14.59%. Selected trajectory solutions

• optical coherences can be adiabatically eliminated;

Part VI

20
The precise object being validated
A model has three distinct tests: whether its equations define an admissible quantum process,
whether its numerical solution approximates those equations, and whether its chosen degrees of
freedom, reservoirs, boundary conditions and input parameters describe the apparatus. Passing one
test does not imply the next. The microscopic construction below supplies frequency-dependent drift,
ordered noise and photocurrent spectra for explicitly specified reduced models. It does not identify
the phenomenological mean-field gains elsewhere in this document with a complete quantum channel.
In particular, a commutator-preserving vacuum completion of an arbitrary drift and diffusion derived
from atomic jumps are different physical models.
The local stationary four-level model, periodically driven four-level model, finite spatial mode
projection, ballistic single-atom trajectory and thermal inflow ensemble each have a different clo-

Validation hierarchy
Agreement between this algebra and the production program is an algebraic and numerical con-

Conclusions and validation status
The stationary resolvent, pole expansion with its defective-generator qualification, entire Lyapunov
solution and post-cell detection map supply the analytic core. The microscopic extension derives
ordered diffusion from GKSL jumps and checks it independently by QRT; it retains periodic cross-
harmonic correlations, the physical sideband space, transverse phases, finite-age boundary noise and
Poisson arrival noise. These are explicit physical constraints on reduced models, not fitted squeezing
formulas.
Admissibility and numerical accuracy remain separate. Positive density matrices do not certify
Floquet convergence; PSD noise does not certify commutators or channel CP; accurate individual
trajectories do not certify a thermal integral.
The coarse thermal samples in Table 5 fail their
declared agreement gate. Their injected internal state is an additional boundary assumption, and a
nonlocal Maxwell solution is required to turn transported atomic kernels into cell-output fields.
The phenomenological gain model substantially disagrees with measured output powers at the
documented experimental setting. Conditional quantum results from a reciprocal four-state model
use a different normalization and declared inputs and do not remove that discrepancy by themselves.
A predictive apparatus spectrum requires a justified Zeeman/polarization reduction, independently
characterized density and reservoirs, physically prepared inflow, converged thermal and spatial inte-
grals, a solved optical boundary problem, and measured collection and detector functions. The pole,
Doppler and conditional noise examples in this document constrain that work; none establishes the
experimental squeezing bandwidth or noise level.

Four-level model limitations and unresolved physics
The four-hyperfine-level model is suitable for qualitative gain structure, reduced hyperfine-scale
intuition, and comparison with the existing reduced simulation. It is not automatically suﬀicient for
absolute squeezing, polarization-sensitive effects, magnetic-field response, quantitative spontaneous-
emission noise, or accurate Raman linewidths.
The following physics is absent from, or not jointly established in, a converged apparatus model.
Some items are included conditionally in the reduced constructions of Part VI:
• polarization-dependent Clebsch–Gordan pathways and explicit σ± and π selection rules;
49

85Rb double-Λ FWM
theory and precision
• Zeeman optical pumping, ground-state orientation and alignment, dark-state formation, and
magnetic-field-dependent coherences;
• interference among distinct mF pathways and polarization-dependent spontaneous emission;
• spatial pump depletion, longitudinal attenuation, transverse Gaussian averaging, diffraction,
mode mismatch, and cell-window etalons;
• buffer-gas or wall-collision dynamics, transit-time distributions, radiation trapping, and non-
Markovian reservoirs;
• multidimensional velocity-changing collisions, beam angular distributions, and promotion of the
separate non-collinear Raman-Doppler reference into the finite-seed segmented production path;
• a converged apparatus-level microscopic diffusion and collected fluorescence model; reduced-
model jump diffusion is derived in Part VI;
• detector transfer functions, electronic cross-talk, common-mode rejection, and the apparatus-
specific 2 MHz laser-source noise peak; and
• an independently measured SQL calibration and gain definition matched exactly to the theoretical
modes.
None of these can be assumed small. The gain discrepancy of Table 20 and the 1.36–1.38 MHz
angular Doppler scale, which is comparable to the power-broadened Raman pole width itself, are
order-unity effects; the present calculation does not yet define a squeezing-bandwidth prediction.

Implementation notes for the analytic layers
The following are the practical requirements implied by Secs. 10 and 18.
1. Do not diagonalize Mq without a guard. Use Eq. (62) by default. If Eq. (58) is used for
interpretation, check cond(S) and fall back when it exceeds a threshold; the two forms agree to
10−15 in the well-conditioned regime.
2. Assert the invariants. W must be Hermitian and positive semidefinite for any Hermitian,
positive-semidefinite D, and W(0) = 0. These are cheap tests that catch sign and conjugation
errors in the creation/annihilation bookkeeping.
3. Treat the stationary subspace explicitly at zero frequency. For Ω̸= 0 the unique trace-
mode residue vanishes by Eq. (26). At Ω= 0, solve on the trace-zero subspace or use the Drazin
inverse; do not evaluate a formal 0/0 term. If more than one stationary mode exists, project the
full stationary subspace and check every zero-mode residue.
4. Track residues, not only eigenvalues.
A narrow pole with a negligible residue is not a
visible spectral feature. Ranking poles by |Am|/Γm rather than by Γm alone identifies which
ones actually shape S−(ΩSA).
5. Recompute K after any normalization change. Equation (50) is contracted with J and is
therefore basis dependent through Eq. (7).
6. Report the analysis frequency with every spectrum. The ΩSA dependence enters both Mq
and D; a spectrum quoted without its frequency grid cannot be compared with a measurement.
